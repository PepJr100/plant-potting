# Substrate Science

The scientific foundation of the recommendation engine. These ten sources establish the physical, chemical, and biological vocabulary of soilless substrates: pore-space geometry, water-holding capacity, cation exchange, pH chemistry, and the component-by-component property tables that any substrate-formulation logic must be grounded in. Together they cover (1) the academic theory of root-zone management, (2) extension-service component data with hard numbers, (3) crop-specific guidance for the high-value Phalaenopsis market, and (4) the peat-free regulatory and ethical context shaping which substrates can credibly be recommended in 2026.

### Greenhouse Root Substrates (Purdue Extension, M.R. Evans, HO-255-W)
- **Cache file:** `www_extension_purdue_edu_extmedia_HO_HO-255-W_pdf.pdf`
- **Source:** https://www.extension.purdue.edu/extmedia/HO/HO-255-W.pdf
- **Summary:** Purdue Extension guide covering all major soilless substrate components with precise physical and chemical data for each. Details sphagnum peat, coir, composted barks, perlite, pumice, vermiculite, parboiled rice hulls (PBH), rockwool, calcined clay, sand, and amendments. Explains substrate design philosophy, mixing protocols, and storage/handling best practices.
- **Key concepts:** sphagnum peat, coir, composted bark, perlite, vermiculite, parboiled rice hulls, rockwool, calcined clay, air-filled pore space, water-holding capacity, bulk density, CEC, electrical conductivity, wetting agents
- **Notable data:**
  - Sphagnum peat: pore space up to 97%, WHC 60–68% vol, bulk density ~0.1 g/cm³, pH 3.0–4.5, CEC 90–140 meq/100g
  - Coir: pH 5.8–6.9, WHC 73–80% vol, air-filled pores 9.5–12.5%, CEC 39–60 meq/100g
  - Perlite: total pore space 63%, WHC 38%, air-filled pore space 25%
  - PBH: bulk density 0.1 g/cm³, total pore space ~90%, air-filled pore space ~69%
  - Rockwool granular: total pore space 91%, WHC 65%, air-filled pore space 25%
  - Most greenhouse crops: pH 5.4–6.6 in soilless substrate
- **See also:** Greenhouse Root Substrates — U Arkansas Unit 7 (this file, same author, expanded); Properties of Growing Media — Greenhouse Management Chapter 12 (this file); Plant pH Preferences — UConn (this file)

### IPA Substrates for Orchids (International Phalaenopsis Alliance)
- **Cache file:** `staugorchidsociety_org_PDF_IPASubstrates_pdf.pdf`
- **Source:** https://staugorchidsociety.org/PDF/IPASubstrates.pdf
- **Summary:** IPA technical document on substrate selection for orchid cultivation, comparing bark mixes, sphagnum moss, semi-hydroponic systems, and inorganic media. Addresses unique aeration requirements of epiphytic orchid roots and how substrates affect root health and repotting frequency. Crucial scaffolding for the orchid branch of the recommendation engine.
- **Key concepts:** epiphytic orchids, bark substrate, sphagnum moss, semi-hydroponics, LECA, inorganic media, root aeration, repotting frequency, Phalaenopsis, Cattleya, moisture retention
- **Notable data:**
  - Orchid substrates require air-filled pore space >30% due to epiphytic root physiology
  - Bark-based mixes decompose over 1–3 years, requiring periodic repotting
  - Sphagnum holds significantly more water than bark; risks overwatering
  - LECA provides consistent moisture and air simultaneously
- **See also:** Substrate for Phalaenopsis — Anthura (this file); Plant pH Preferences — UConn (this file, orchid pH 4.5–5.5); Best Growing Medium Mixes for Orchid Families — Dark Orchid (`plant-profiles.md`); Orchid Bark Guide — Harwoods (`plant-profiles.md`)

### Air Porosity and Water-Holding Ability of Media Components (Sun Gro Horticulture)
- **Cache file:** `www_sungro_com_air-porosity-and-water-holding-ability-of-media-components.html`
- **Source:** https://www.sungro.com/air-porosity-and-water-holding-ability-of-media-components/
- **Summary:** Industry practitioner article from North America's largest growing media manufacturer explaining how particle size, pore distribution, and component selection determine air porosity and water-holding capacity. Covers macropores vs. micropores, the three stages of water-holding capacity (saturation, container capacity, wilting point), and the perched water table effect in containers. Provides practical formulation examples.
- **Key concepts:** macropores, micropores, total porosity, container capacity, perched water table, air space, bulk density, particle size, compaction, pre-wetting, available water
- **Notable data:**
  - Soilless media normal range: total porosity 75–95%, air space 10–30%, container capacity 65–80%
  - Recommended air-space: propagation 10–15%, general greenhouse 15–25%, perennial ≤25%
  - High-bark medium (50% peat / 10% perlite / 15% vermiculite / 25% bark): porosity 87%, container capacity 71%, air space 16%
  - Low-bark medium (55% peat / 15% perlite / 15% vermiculite / 15% bark): porosity 90%, container capacity 78%, air space 12%
- **See also:** Greenhouse Root Substrates — Purdue HO-255-W (this file); Properties of Growing Media — Greenhouse Management Chapter 12 (this file); Aroid Substrate Guide — Foliage Factory (`plant-profiles.md`)

### Properties of Growing Media (Greenhouse Management, Chapter 12)
- **Cache file:** `www_greenhouse-management_com_greenhouse_management_growing_media_greenhouse_crops_properties_growing_media_htm.htm`
- **Source:** https://www.greenhouse-management.com/…/properties_growing_media.htm
- **Summary:** Textbook chapter defining bulk density, WHC, air-filled porosity, media stability, CEC, pH, EC, and C:N ratio as the core measurable substrate parameters. Introduces each with clear definitions and practical implications for containerised plant production. Functions as the canonical glossary for substrate properties.
- **Key concepts:** bulk density, WHC, air-filled porosity, total porosity, CEC, substrate pH, electrical conductivity, C:N ratio, macropores, media stability, sphagnum peat, perlite, coir, vermiculite
- **Notable data:**
  - Optimal pH: 6.2–6.8 (soil-based), 5.4–6.6 (soilless)
  - High C:N ratio → risk of nitrogen immobilisation by microorganisms
  - Acid substrate: Ca, Mg, P, B, Mo deficient; Al and Mn excessive
  - Alkaline substrate: P, Fe, Cu, Zn, B frequently deficient
- **See also:** Greenhouse Root Substrates — Purdue HO-255-W (this file); Plant pH Preferences — UConn (this file); Soilless Culture (Raviv & Lieth) (this file)

### Physico-Chemical and Physical Properties of Some Substrates Used in Horticulture (Sahin, Anapali & Ercisli, 2002 — *Gartenbauwissenschaft* 67(2): 55–60)
- **Cache file:** `www_researchgate_net_publication_271177653_Physico-chemical_and_physical_properties_of_some_substrates_used_in_horticult.pdf` (duplicate at `…_390201538_….pdf` — same paper, two ResearchGate publication IDs)
- **Source:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271177653_Physico-chemical_and_physical_properties_of_some_substrates_used_in_horticulture · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390201538_Physico-chemical_and_physical_properties_of_some_substrates_used_in_horticulture
- **Summary:** Atatürk University study (Erzurum, Turkey, 2001 fieldwork, 2002 publication) measuring pH, EC, CEC, carbonates, organic matter, particle-size distribution, bulk density, water-retention curves, and pore-size distribution for six pure substrates (peat moss, peat, sawdust, perlite, pumice, creek sand) and fourteen 1:1 binary mixes. Adds Turkish pumice and perlite to the substrate-properties literature alongside the Purdue and U-Arkansas extension data. Particularly useful because it directly measures the same parameter set across a wide ingredient × mix matrix, so the team can read off "what does pumice + peat at 1:1 actually do" rather than estimating from pure-ingredient data.
- **Key concepts:** pumice, perlite, peat moss, sawdust, creek sand, 1:1 binary substrate mixes, pH, EC, CEC, organic matter, bulk density, water retention at low tensions (pF < 2.52), pore-size distribution (macro/meso/micro/ultramicropores), Turkish pumice and perlite reserves
- **Notable data:**
  - **Pure-substrate pH range:** 5.1 (peat moss, peat) → 7.6 (pumice)
  - **EC range:** 0.096 dS/m (pumice) → 1.065 dS/m (peat)
  - **CEC range:** ~0 (perlite, no surface charge) → 206.4 cmol/kg (peat moss); peat moss ~250 cmol/kg theoretical OM ceiling
  - **Organic matter:** peat moss 95.0 %, peat 41.7 %, sawdust 39.2 %; perlite/pumice/creek sand all ~0 %
  - **Bulk density (g/cm³):** peat moss 0.086 → creek sand 1.491; mixes: peat moss + perlite 0.099 (lightest), pumice + creek sand 1.081 (heaviest)
  - **Water held at low tension (<pF 2.52):** pumice 62.6 %, sawdust 59.2 %, peat moss 57.1 %, perlite 53.7 %, peat 53.0 %, creek sand 28.9 %; peat:sawdust 60.0 % (best organic-organic mix), sawdust:perlite 57.2 %, perlite:creek sand 40.1 %
  - **Macropore content (>100 µm, drainage/aeration):** pumice 60.2 %, sawdust 56.9 %, peat:sawdust 56.0 %, sawdust:perlite 52.6 %, perlite:creek sand 34.4 %
  - Carbonates < 1 % for all substrates ("low-carbonate" classification)
- **See also:** Soilless Culture — Raviv & Lieth (this file); Greenhouse Root Substrates — Purdue HO-255-W (this file); Greenhouse Root Substrates — U Arkansas Unit 7 (this file); Air Porosity and Water-Holding Ability — Sun Gro (this file); Properties of Growing Media — Greenhouse Management Chapter 12 (this file); DIY aroid mix recipes (`plant-profiles.md`, which use the same ingredients in different ratios)
